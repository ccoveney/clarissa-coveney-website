About Me

I am a K99/R00-funded postdoctoral researcher in Dr. Terence Capellini’s laboratory at Harvard University. My research integrates mechanobiology, evolutionary biology, functional genomics, and epigenetics to understand how genetic and environmental factors shape musculoskeletal development, skeletal morphology, and disease risk.

My current work focuses on understanding how genetic variants—particularly non-coding variants—regulate gene expression in a context-dependent manner. I use high-throughput functional genomic approaches, including Massively Parallel Reporter Assays, mouse models, and humanized variants, to investigate how regulatory elements influence joint morphology, cartilage homeostasis, and musculoskeletal disease.

My long-term research goal is to understand how mechanical forces shape the genomics of skeletal form and disease.

Research

My research program is centered on mechanoepigenetics: understanding how mechanical forces at the tissue and extracellular matrix levels influence chromatin structure, enhancer activity, gene expression, and disease risk.

Mechanoepigenetics

How do mechanical forces influence chromatin structure, enhancer activity, and gene expression in musculoskeletal tissues?

Regulatory Genomics

How do non-coding regulatory elements shape joint development, morphology, cartilage homeostasis, and osteoarthritis risk?

Evolutionary Genetics

How have ancient genetic variants contributed to variation in modern human skeletal biology and disease susceptibility?

My work combines mouse models, humanized variants, Massively Parallel Reporter Assays, single-cell multiomics, spatial transcriptomics, chromatin conformation assays, and biomechanical approaches.
